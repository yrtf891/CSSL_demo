Abstract

Contrast class-wise patches, and let their separation set the noise

Semi-supervised segmentation reduces the expert annotation that medical images require. We present a contrastive method in which every window of an image contributes one embedding per class, pooled from the encoder features of the unmasked image, and in which the rims of touching nuclei form a contrastive class of their own. A supervised contrastive loss with synthesized hard negatives separates these embeddings, and their Silhouette coefficient sets the noise power of a feature-perturbation consistency loss. The network is thus asked to tolerate strong feature noise only once its representation separates the classes, closing a feedback loop that fixed-strength perturbation methods lack. Added to a strong weak-to-strong pseudo-labelling recipe and evaluated under published protocols, the method reaches a nucleus F1 score of 80.87 on MoNuSeg with half of the labels, above Self-Loop, above the recipe on every label split, and above a fully supervised model. On T2-weighted abdominal magnetic resonance images from CHAOS with a quarter of the labels, it reaches a Dice score of 89.01, seven points above DAFNet.

New unit of contrast

Class-wise patches with feature-level pooling

Each embedding pools the features of the unmasked image over one class in one window. Unlike pixel- or region-level contrast, it carries local context without an extra encoder pass, and the encoder only ever sees real images.

New result

Mixed negatives are provably harder

For any mixing weight in (0, 1], a synthesized negative is strictly more similar to its anchor than the real negative it comes from (Lemma 1), so it sharpens the contrast exactly where classes are confused.

The method in five animations

One training step of CSSL, from pseudo-labels to Silhouette-controlled noise

Every animation uses real images and real features of the trained network. Grey parts are the weak-to-strong recipe; CSSL adds the contrastive branch and the Silhouette feedback on top.

Step 0 · Recipe

Weak-to-strong pseudo-labelling

An exponential-moving-average teacher labels a weak view; two CutMix strong views learn from its confident pixels. This recipe is identical in every run, so every gain below is CSSL's.

ŷ = argmax fξ(uw),   M = 1[max q ≥ 0.95]
Step 1 · Class-wise patches

One patch per class and window

Every 64 × 64 window yields a patch for each class with at least 25 pixels. Nucleus rims within two pixels of the background form a class of their own, which is where merged nuclei are decided.

Step 2 · Feature-level pooling

Encode once, pool by class fraction

The full, unmasked image is encoded a single time. Each patch embedding pools the encoder cells under its window, weighted by the fraction of each cell its class covers.

z = norm(φ(Σc m̄(c) hc(x) / Σc m̄(c)))
Step 3 · Contrast

Pull classes together, push them apart

A supervised contrastive loss with a memory of 4096 past embeddings and up to 256 synthesized hard negatives per anchor separates the classes. The panel tracks the Silhouette ω as the clusters form.

Step 4 · Silhouette feedback

Perturb only as hard as the embedding allows

The running Silhouette sets the noise level λ of a feature-perturbation consistency loss on every encoder level. Mixed classes get mild noise; separated classes get strong noise.

λ = (ωsmoothed + 1)/2 + 0.05,   h̃ = h + √λ σ(h) ξ
Interactive · Property 1

Separate the classes and watch the noise grow

Drag the slider to move three classes apart. The page computes the real Silhouette coefficient of these points with the Manhattan distance, maps it to the noise level λ exactly as CSSL does, and perturbs a feature map with noise √λ σ(h) ξ. The signal-to-noise ratio is −10 log10 λ, so it falls by more than 13 dB from mixed to separated classes.

Property 1. The expected noise power equals λ times the variance of each feature map, at every layer and activation scale. Hence the SNR moves from 13.0 dB (smoothed ω = −1) through 2.6 dB (ω = 0) to −0.2 dB (ω = 1).

Interactive · Lemma 1

Mixed negatives are always harder

A synthesized negative mixes the anchor zi with a real negative zn and projects back to the sphere. It slides along the great circle towards the anchor, so its similarity to the anchor is strictly higher than its parent's for every mixing weight α in (0, 1]. CSSL draws α from U(0, 0.4), the shaded arc.

Lemma 1. For unit vectors zn ≠ ±zi and α ∈ (0, 1], z̃ = norm(α zi + (1 − α) zn) satisfies ziᵀz̃ > ziᵀzn. The proof is three lines in the paper; here you can try to break it.

Ablations

The contrastive branch carries the method

Δ is the full model minus the model without a component, on one split per dataset; positive means the component helps. Removing the contrastive loss costs 2.15 points on MoNuSeg and 7.21 on CHAOS, and the boundary class alone is worth 2.67.

Reproducibility

Every number has a log behind it

The anonymous repository holds the code, the configuration and training log of every run, and scripts that print every number in the tables. Each final model was tested once; no test-time augmentation or post-processing.
