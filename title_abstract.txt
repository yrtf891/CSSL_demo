Title:
Silhouette-Adaptive Contrastive Learning With Class-Wise Patches for Semi-Supervised Medical Image Segmentation

Abstract (169 words):
Semi-supervised segmentation reduces the expert annotation that medical images require. We present a contrastive method in which every window of an image contributes one embedding per class, pooled from the encoder features of the unmasked image, and in which the rims of touching nuclei form a contrastive class of their own. A supervised contrastive loss with synthesized hard negatives separates these embeddings, and their Silhouette coefficient sets the noise power of a feature-perturbation consistency loss. The network is thus asked to tolerate strong feature noise only once its representation separates the classes, closing a feedback loop that fixed-strength perturbation methods lack. Added to a strong weak-to-strong pseudo-labelling recipe and evaluated under published protocols, the method reaches a nucleus F1 score of 80.87 on MoNuSeg with half of the labels, above Self-Loop, above the recipe on every label split, and above a fully supervised model. On T2-weighted abdominal magnetic resonance images from CHAOS with a quarter of the labels, it reaches a Dice score of 89.01, seven points above DAFNet.

Index Terms:
Consistency regularization, contrastive learning, medical image segmentation, semi-supervised learning, Silhouette coefficient.
